## Why Prune Tomato Plants?

Pruning can help improve airflow and make a tomato plant easier to manage.

[Read our guide to growing tomatoes in pots](/blog/how-to-grow-tomatoes-in-pots/).

## How to Prune Tomato Plants

Start by looking at the side growth on the plant. A sucker is a small shoot that grows in the joint between the main stem and a leaf branch. It often points upwards from this V-shaped gap.

![Removing a tomato sucker](/images/growing-guides/test-text-content-revision/test-text-content-revision-2-pruning.webp)

To prune a tomato plant, follow these simple steps:

1. Check the plant from the bottom upwards and look for small suckers in the leaf joints.
2. Pinch out small suckers with your fingers, or use clean snips for thicker growth.
3. Remove only a few suckers at a time, especially if the plant is young or looks weak.
4. Leave plenty of healthy leaves on the plant so it can keep making energy and shade its fruit.

Do not remove every sucker or strip too many leaves at once. Over-pruning can stress the plant and reduce its leaf cover, which may make it weaker and leave fruit more exposed to strong sun.

## After Pruning

Water the plant normally and check it over again a few days later.
